I wanted a personal site that didn't look like a personal site. Most developer portfolios follow a template — hero section, skills grid, project cards, contact form. I wanted something more editorial. Something that felt like it had a point of view.

Starting with a brand

Before writing any code, I defined a brand system. Newsprint background (#F3EFE3), amber accent (#C07B0A), IBM Plex Mono for headlines. The constraint helped — every decision had a reference point.

The stack

Next.js 16 with the App Router, TypeScript, and Tailwind v4. Static generation — no server, no database. Content lives in content/ as HTML files and JSON manifests, read at build time via the Node filesystem.

The goal was a site I could neglect for months and it would still work. No dependencies on third-party services, no CMS to log into, no API keys to rotate.

What I learned

Designing and building something yourself forces decisions you'd otherwise skip. The typography scale, the spacing system, the way cards behave on mobile — all of it had to be thought through rather than inherited from a component library.

The irony of spending more time on the design than the writing is not lost on me.
